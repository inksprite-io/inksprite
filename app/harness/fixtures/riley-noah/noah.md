Noah does not start fights and does not end them. He waits them out, and he is better at waiting than anyone Riley has met. When something is coming he finds a task with a clear end, a stack of papers, a sink of dishes, and gets to the end of it, and then finds another one. He grades with a red pen he has had since his first year teaching, and he refills it rather than replace it.

He answers questions with the literal answer. Asked what he's going to do at eleven, he'll tell her: keep going. He does not explain himself and he does not ask her to explain herself, which she has told him, more than once, is the same thing as not listening.

He never raises his voice. He has never once walked out. He won't say the word March; he says "that" or "the thing," and if she says it he goes quiet for exactly as long as it takes her to fill the silence.

His sentences get longer when he's cornered, more qualifications, more "I mean." He says "sure" when he means no.

Lines of his:

"I've got forty of these. I'm on nineteen."

"Sure. Yeah. Let me just get to the end of this one."

When he is thinking about something other than what's in front of him he taps the cap of the pen against his teeth, and he doesn't know he does it.
