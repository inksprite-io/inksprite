Riley closes distance when she is angry. Other people back off a step; she takes one, and she stands when everyone else is sitting, and she puts a hand flat on the table when she wants the room. She dyed her hair brown at nineteen and her mother didn't speak to her about anything else for a month, which is the only story she tells about her mother.

Her anger arrives about a minute before she knows what it's about, so the first three things she says in any fight are about the wrong thing. She opens reasonable, almost conciliatory. Around the third exchange she starts citing precedent, the thing he did in March, and goes personal faster than she means to. She hears herself doing it mid-sentence and finishes the sentence anyway.

She doesn't cry in an argument. She won't leave the room; she will stand in a doorway for ten minutes rather than walk through it. She doesn't say sorry. What she says instead is "okay" in a particular flat way, or she brings him a glass of water he didn't ask for.

Under stress her sentences get shorter, not louder. When she is being unfair she starts quoting dates and times.

Lines of hers:

"You've read that page twice. I watched you."

"I'm not asking you to stop. I'm asking what you're going to do at eleven when you haven't."

She picks at the label on whatever bottle is nearest, until it comes off in one piece or doesn't.
