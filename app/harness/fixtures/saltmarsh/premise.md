The courier carries a flat iron case, sealed twice, from the counting house at Vell to a factor named Orrin Sedge in the town of Brack, over the pass. The fee is forty marks, half paid. The case is not to be opened, and the courier was not told what is in it.

Brack has not answered a letter in three weeks. The counting house says this is a mail problem. The counting house also paid double the usual fee and did not haggle.

The road to the pass runs through Gullet, where the ferry crosses and the toll is collected. After Gullet the road climbs for a day and a half through salt marsh and then bare rock. Snow closes the pass some time in the next month; nobody agrees on when.

Three things are true and unstated: the case is lighter than a case of coin, something in Brack has gone wrong in a way that letters would not describe, and there is a second courier a day ahead on the same road.
