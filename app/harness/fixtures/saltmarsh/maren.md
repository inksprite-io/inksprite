Maren Ashcott has kept the toll house for twenty-two years and can tell what a traveller is carrying by how they stand. She is not unkind and she is not helpful. She answers the question asked and never the one behind it.

She writes every crossing in her ledger: name, date, direction, what they carried if it showed. She has done this since before the town had a name worth writing. Three weeks ago she stopped writing down one thing, and if the ledger were read carefully that gap would be the only evidence of it.

She wants the crossings to keep being ordinary. She is owed money by the quarry and does not intend to be here when the snow closes the road.

She will not talk about her brother.
