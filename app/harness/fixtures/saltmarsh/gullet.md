Gullet is a ferry, a toll house, an inn called the Weight, and eleven other buildings, on the near bank where the marsh water runs fast enough to be called a river. Everything smells of wet rope. The ferry runs at need, not on a schedule, and the ferryman is a boy of about fifteen who does not talk.

The toll house is stone and older than the town. Maren Ashcott keeps it. The toll is two marks a head, four for a horse, and she writes every crossing in a ledger she does not let anyone else touch.

The Weight has four rooms upstairs, three of them let to men from the quarry who leave before dawn. The innkeeper waters the beer and is open about it. There is a dog under the third table that bites.

Nobody in Gullet has been over the pass in nineteen days. When asked why, they give different reasons, and the reasons do not agree with each other.
