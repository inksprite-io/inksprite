# Interview with Priya

2026-08-22, forty minutes, Dana. Priya is a researcher with about eight thousand notes going back six years.

## How she searches now

She searches by title, and when that fails she scrolls by date, which she describes as "guessing which month I was thinking about it." Perhaps a third of her searches fail on the first try.

## What she searches for

- **A phrase she remembers.** "I know I wrote 'the deadline is a fiction' somewhere." She wants the note that contains those words, exactly. This is the most common kind and the one she is most annoyed by when it fails.
- **A person or a project.** A name, a codeword. Short queries, one or two words.
- **A topic she cannot name.** "The thing about how the model was overfitting to the small set." She does not have the words the note uses.

## What she expects

Results as she types, like the title search does. She would not wait for a search button. She also expects the newest matching note first when several match equally, because "the recent one is the one I meant."

## What she would not accept

Anything that sends her notes off the laptop. She asked about this unprompted.

## Quotes

- "If I type the exact words and it doesn't find it, I stop trusting it."
- "Half the time I don't know what I called it. I know what it was about."

## For the design

The exact-phrase path is non-negotiable and has to be fast. The topic path is where dense retrieval earns its place. Recency as a tiebreak is cheap and expected.
