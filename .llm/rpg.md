# Adventure Mode

## Concept

The core concept is a structured AI-powered role playing game, similar to a TTRPG. The AI will act as a game master, and will use a variety of tools to generate a world and story. The TTRPG engine will ground the game in a structured set of rules and systems. The idea is to achieve the freedom of a TTRPG sandbox as well as the structure of an RPG rule system. The AI game master will enable emergent story telling and allow the player to think outside the box and break the constraints of a traditional RPG video game.

## Core Rules

Adventure mode will be a structured role playing game based on OSR TTRPGs. I'm planning to use the openly licensed (OGL) material that B/X retroclones such as Old-School Essentials publish, with modifications, and nothing that isn't under an open license. I'll need to make substantial changes for this to work well as a single player video game instead of a party-based TTRPG.

Major changes:
- Characters need more utility in order to solve problems on their own. e.g. a magic user will be unable to make progress if they can't deal with traps. I'll likely need to borrow some of the skills from later additions.
- Ascending AC for familiarity
- Separate race and class.
- More survivability. I'll likely need to increase hit dies by one step, similar to 5e.

It may end up looking like a mix of B/X and 3.5 or 5e. However, ideally I'll maintain compatibility with B/X and similar systems.

## Interface

The main interface will consist of:
- A main text window, which is where in-game descriptions and dialog will be presented to the player.
- A chat window, which is where the player can talk to the AI game master, ask questions, and describe what they want to do. The player will use this similar to how they would talk to a real game master, e.g. "I search the room for traps".
- A map, which will show the player's position in the world, the environment around them, NPCs, etc.
- Traditional RPG menus and interfaces for managing: inventory, characters, spells, quests, shops, etc.
- Optionally, combat or chat interfaces that the player can use instead of describing their actions to the GM.
- Eventually, maybe generated character portraits.

## Gameplay

Game play will have elements of a freeform text adventure, TTRPG, and old school rogue-likes. Worlds and quests will be randomly generated, and the character will move around the world similar to in rogue. However, combat will be similar to a TTRPG. Purely text based, with no combat character movement.

Players will be able to interact with the world in a freeform way. e.g. they can have conversations with NPCs, or interact with the environment in unexpected ways. e.g. if they can't unlock a door, they might stick something in the keyhole and cast "enlarge" to break the mechanism. The AI GM will mediate these interactions.

## World Generation

Rather than relying on pure text completion for story and world generation, we'll use a set of structured tools. For example:
- Name generation tools to avoid repetitive names generated by AI.
- We'll provide the AI GM with an oracle that can be used to test assumptions, e.g. the AI GM can ask "Is the chest trapped? (Unlikely)" to determine how the scene should proceed.
- We'll also draw a tarot card, the way solo-RPG oracles do, to provide story hints. e.g. we draw the Five of Pentacles (loss, hardship). This might prompt the AI to present a situation where the player must choose between abandoning a piece of gear to escape danger, or a set piece encounter where a caravan abandons their supplies to escape a goblin ambush.
- World and dungeon generation tools, similar to rogue and dwarf fortress
- History and lore generation tools to help the AI generate a believable world
- We'll write our own encounter tables for the AI to roll on, in the spirit of the old-school ones: who or what is met, how many, and how they react.
