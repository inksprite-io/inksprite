A character card is a character packaged to share: who they are, the scene
they open with, and often a lorebook, the world they live in. inksprite imports
the cards made for SillyTavern and the sites that share them, and plays them as
cooperative storytelling: you play your character, the model plays everyone
else and the world, and either of you can steer.

This guide plays [Pif](https://chub.ai/characters/bipbop/pif-2b066033), by
bipbop: an orphan mechanic in a world of flying islands who takes odd jobs
from the underworld. The game was played with Claude Sonnet 5.5, through
[Anthropic](/providers/#anthropic-and-openai).

## Turn on Roleplay

Roleplay is an extension, off until you turn it on, so someone using
inksprite only to write never sees it. Open **Settings** › **Extensions** and
switch on **Roleplay**. That brings the card library, character cards in
**Import**, and the Roleplay profiles.

## Get the card and its lorebook

On [Pif's page](https://chub.ai/characters/bipbop/pif-2b066033), download the
card as a PNG: the picture carries the card inside it. The notes under it link
two lorebooks; download the first, for SillyTavern
([sohhf2.json](https://files.catbox.moe/sohhf2.json)). A card can come as JSON
instead, and imports the same way.

## Import them

Make a project for the game. Right-click it, choose **Import** › **Files…**,
and choose the PNG. inksprite says what's in the card before it writes
anything. Click **Import**, then do the same with the lorebook.

<Screenshot
  name="roleplay-import-card"
  alt="Importing Pif: a description, a greeting and post-history instructions"
/>

Both go in a **Cards** folder: the card in **Characters**, a document for each
part of it, and the lorebook in **Lorebooks**, a document for each of its 246
entries. The model reads folder names too, so right-click the lorebook's
folder, named after its file, choose **Rename**, and call it
**Divided Skies**, the name of Pif's world.

<Screenshot
  name="roleplay-outline"
  alt="The card and the lorebook in the outline"
/>

## Make a persona

Your character is a document, like everything else the model reads. Make a
folder called **Personas**, and a document in it named for your character,
with a description. This guide plays Tal:

> A tall, lean man who looks to be in his late 30s. He speaks little and wears
> a pair of revolvers at his hip.

## Start the chat

Right-click the card's folder, **Pif**, choose **Chat with Pif…**, type your
character's name in **Your name**, and click **Start chat**. The card says
`{{user}}` where it means you, and the model reads your name there instead. A
card with more than one greeting lets you choose one here.

<Screenshot name="roleplay-start-chat" alt="Starting a chat with Pif as Tal" />

The chat opens on the **Roleplay** profile (or the one chosen as **Profile for
character cards** in **Settings** › **Extensions** › **Roleplay**), with Pif's
greeting as its first message.

Now hover over **Tal** in the outline and click the pin. A pinned document
goes to the model with every message you send, so it always knows who you are
playing. The card's description is pinned already; pinned documents show in
colour. [Pins](/chats/#pin-documents) belong to the chat they were made in.

<Screenshot
  name="roleplay-pinned"
  alt="Tal pinned to the chat, and Pif's greeting"
/>

## Turn on the dice

Click the gear in the message box for the chat's settings. Under
[Roleplay](/chats/#roleplay) are your name and the card's post-history
instructions, which go with every message after the author's note. Change
either here.

<Screenshot
  name="roleplay-settings"
  alt="The chat's Roleplay settings: Tal, Pif, and the card's post-history instructions"
/>

Open **Tools**, and switch on **RPG Tools**: dice, the oracle, tables, tarot
and names. A model like Claude Sonnet 5.5 rolls on its own when an outcome is
in doubt.

**Documents** is on already, so the model looks entries up in the lorebook as
the story needs them, rather than reading all 13,000 words with every message.

<Screenshot
  name="roleplay-tools"
  alt="The chat's tools, with RPG Tools switched on"
/>

## Let the model read up

Before the first move, tell the model what to do. A line that starts with
`OOC:`, out of character, is from you as the player rather than from Tal:

> OOC: Before we start, explore the lorebook in Cards/Lorebooks/Divided Skies
> and gather whatever context you need to run this world. Let me know when
> you're ready.

<Clip
  name="roleplay-explore"
  label="The model lists the lorebook, reads the entries it wants, and says what it found"
/>

It read ten entries (the world, Pif's island, her slum, her rival, the crew
that keeps foiling her) and answered out of character. Click **11 tool calls**
to see what it read.

## Play

Write short turns, the way you'd talk at a table: what Tal says and does.

> "Doesn't seem like much of a choice to me." I draw my revolver and fire.

The model narrates, plays Pif and everyone else, and leaves Tal to you. When
the outcome is in doubt, it rolls: here a d20 for the shot, which came up 13,
a hit that only dents the war-golem.

## Steer the story

### Ask the oracle

The oracle answers a yes-or-no question at the odds you give it. Put it on a
line of its own, then your move on the next (<kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd>
starts a new line):

```
/oracle(likely) Is the boiler vent open when it raises the hammer?
I wait for the hammer to come up and put two rounds in the vent.
```

The odds are `nearly_impossible`, `very_unlikely`, `unlikely`, `fifty_fifty`,
`likely`, `very_likely` or `nearly_certain`; leave them off for 50/50. The
model takes the answer as fact. Here it was no, so the vent was shuttered:

<Screenshot
  name="roleplay-oracle"
  alt="The oracle's answer in Tal's turn, and the reply built on it"
/>

`/roll 2d6+1` rolls dice the same way. Type `/` for the rest.

### Say what you'd like

An out-of-character note can ask for anything: a twist, a character, a change
of pace, or less of something.

```
I step in close and empty the cylinder into the open vent.

OOC: While we're busy with the golem, have Ridge slip in and make off with the crate.
```

Ridge, Pif's rival, came down from the rafters in the same reply. The dice
still had their say: a 6, and the shots went wide.

<Screenshot
  name="roleplay-ooc"
  alt="An action and an out-of-character note in one turn"

## Compact the chat

Every turn sends the whole chat so far, so a long game gets slower and costs
more. Type `/compact` and the model writes a summary of the game to stand in
for the turns before it.

<Screenshot name="roleplay-summary" alt="The summary at the top of the chat" />

The turns stay in the chat for you to read back. Here the next turn went to
the model at 7.8k tokens (**ctx** in its header), where the one before the
summary was 22k.

<Screenshot
  name="roleplay-after-summary"
  alt="The first turn after the summary, at a third of the size"
/>

`/compact(2)` keeps the last two turns as they are, under the summary.
[Compact a long chat](/chats/#compact-a-long-chat) has the other ways.

## Keep your cards in the library

The card library keeps the cards you play outside any project, so you can find
one again and see which is which. Open it with the cards button on the left,
under the chats, and click **+** to add PNG or JSON cards, or drop them in.
Several can go in at once, and a card already there is skipped.

To add a card from the web, click the link button beside **+** and paste a
link: a card's page on botbooru or Chub, Chub's `creator/name`, or a link
straight to a PNG or JSON card. Dragging a link from another tab onto the
library does the same. Links to botbooru only work in the
[desktop app](/desktop/); in a browser, download the card and drop it in.

Search by name or creator, or click the tags under the search to show only the
cards that have them. Type the start of a tag, like `fem`, and the search
offers `tag:female`; pick it to filter by it. Point at a card to read its
creator's notes, and click it to see the whole card.

**New project** makes a project named for the card, puts the card in it, and
asks the questions **Chat with** asks, so you land in a chat with the
character. Once a card has a project of its own, its button opens that project
instead; **New project** is still in the menu beside it, for a fresh start.
**Add to** puts the card in the project open, the same as importing the file.
A card's details say which projects it's in, and open any of them.

**Download** saves the card as a file to share or keep. Right-click a card, or
hold a finger on it, for all of these and **Delete**. **Import** ›
**Character card…** on a folder opens the library to pick one for that
folder.

Cards tagged NSFW or NSFL show their pictures blurred. Click a blurred picture
in a card's details to see it. The eye beside the search turns the blurring off,
and on again.

Deleting a card from the library leaves the projects it went into as they are.
**Delete card library** in **Settings** › **Extensions** › **Roleplay** empties
it, and turning Roleplay off asks whether to.
