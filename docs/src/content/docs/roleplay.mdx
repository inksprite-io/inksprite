A character card is a character packaged to share: who they are, the scene
they open with, and often a lorebook, the world they live in. inksprite imports
the cards made for SillyTavern and the sites that share them, and plays them as
cooperative storytelling: you play your character, the model plays everyone
else and the world, and either of you can steer.

This guide plays [Pif](https://chub.ai/characters/bipbop/pif-2b066033), by
bipbop: an orphan mechanic in a world of flying islands who takes odd jobs
from the underworld. The game below was played with Claude Sonnet 5.5, through
[Anthropic](/getting-started/#anthropic-and-openai).

## Get the card

On [Pif's page](https://chub.ai/characters/bipbop/pif-2b066033), download the
card as a PNG: the picture carries the card inside it. The notes under it link
two lorebooks; download the first, for SillyTavern
([sohhf2.json](https://files.catbox.moe/sohhf2.json)).

A card can come as JSON instead of a PNG, and imports the same way.

## Import them

Make a project for the game. Right-click it, choose **Import** › **Files…**,
and choose the PNG. inksprite says what's in the card before it writes
anything:

<Screenshot name="roleplay-import-card" alt="Importing Pif: a description, a greeting and an author's note" />

Click the **Import** button, then do the same with the lorebook.

Both go in a **Cards** folder: the card in **Characters**, a document for each
part of it, and the lorebook in **Lorebooks**, a document for each of its 246
entries.

The lorebook's folder is named after its file, and the model reads folder names
too. Right-click it, choose **Rename**, and call it **Divided Skies**, the name
of Pif's world.

<Screenshot name="roleplay-outline" alt="The card and the lorebook in the outline" />

## Make a persona

Your character is a document, like everything else the model reads.
Right-click the project, choose **New folder**, and call it **Personas**.
Right-click that, choose **New document**, call it by your character's name,
and describe them. This guide plays Tal:

> A tall, lean man who looks to be in his late 30s. He speaks little and wears
> a pair of revolvers at his hip.

<Clip name="roleplay-persona" label="Making a Personas folder and Tal's document" />

## Start the chat

Right-click the card's folder, **Pif**, and choose **Chat with Pif…**. Type
your character's name: the card says `{{user}}` where it means you, and this
is what the model reads there instead. A card with more than one greeting
shows them here to choose from; Pif has one.

<Screenshot name="roleplay-start-chat" alt="Starting a chat with Pif as Tal" />

The chat opens on the **Roleplay** profile, with Pif's greeting as its first
message.

### Pin your persona

Hover over **Tal** in the outline and click the pin. A pinned document goes to
the model whole with every message you send, so it always knows who you are
playing, however long the game runs. The card's description is pinned already;
pinned documents show in colour. A pin belongs to the chat it was made in.

<Screenshot name="roleplay-pinned" alt="Tal pinned to the chat, and Pif's greeting" />

## Choose the tools

Click the gear in the message box for the chat's settings, and open **Tools**.

- **Documents** is how the model reads the project. The lorebook is nearly
  13,000 words, more than is worth sending with every message, so the model
  looks entries up when the story needs them. Leave **Read**, **Describe**,
  **List** and **Search** on, and switch off the rest, which write, so the game
  can't change the lorebook.
- **RPG Tools** are dice, the oracle, tables, tarot and names. Switch them on.
  A model like Claude Sonnet 5.5 rolls on its own when an outcome is in doubt.

<Screenshot name="roleplay-tools" alt="The chat's tools: reading the documents, and the RPG tools" />

## Let the model read up

Before the first move, tell the model what to do. A line that starts with
`OOC:`, out of character, is from you as the player rather than from Tal:

> OOC: Before we start, explore the lorebook in Cards/Lorebooks/Divided Skies
> and gather whatever context you need to run this world. Let me know when
> you're ready.

<Clip name="roleplay-explore" label="The model lists the lorebook, reads the entries it wants, and says what it found" />

It listed the lorebook, read ten entries (the world, Pif's island, her slum,
her rival, the crew that keeps foiling her) and answered out of character with
what it took from them. Click **11 tool calls** to see each call and what came
back. It reads more as the story goes somewhere new.

## Play

Write short turns, the way you'd talk at a table: what Tal says and does.

> "Doesn't seem like much of a choice to me." I draw my revolver and fire.

The model narrates what happens, plays Pif and everyone else, and leaves Tal
to you. When the outcome is in doubt, it rolls: here a d20 for the shot, which
came up 13, a hit that only dents the war-golem.

<Screenshot name="roleplay-dice" alt="A turn, and the d20 the model rolled for it" />

## Steer the story

The model doesn't decide the story alone. Two ways to take a hand in it:

### Ask the oracle

The oracle answers a yes-or-no question at the odds you give it. Type it on a
line of its own, then your move on the next (<kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd>
starts a new line):

```
/oracle(likely) Is the boiler vent open when it raises the hammer?
I wait for the hammer to come up and put two rounds in the vent.
```

The odds are `nearly_impossible`, `very_unlikely`, `unlikely`, `fifty_fifty`,
`likely`, `very_likely` or `nearly_certain`; leave them off for 50/50. The
answer is part of your turn, and the model takes it as fact. Here it was no, at
likely odds, so the vent was shuttered:

<Screenshot name="roleplay-oracle" alt="The oracle's answer in Tal's turn, and the reply built on it" />

`/roll` rolls dice the same way, as `/roll 2d6+1`. Type `/` for the rest.

### Say what you'd like

An out-of-character note can ask for anything: a twist, a character, a change
of pace, or less of something.

```
I step in close and empty the cylinder into the open vent.

OOC: While we're busy with the golem, have Ridge slip in and make off with the crate.
```

Ridge, Pif's rival, came down from the rafters in the same reply. The dice
still had their say: a 6, and the shots went wide.

<Screenshot name="roleplay-ooc" alt="An action and an out-of-character note in one turn" />

## Compact the chat

Every turn sends the whole chat so far, so a long game gets slower and costs
more, and can grow past what the model can read at once. Type `/compact` and
the model writes a summary of the game to stand in for the turns before it: who
is who, each scene, and a few lines from each, word for word.

<Screenshot name="roleplay-summary" alt="The summary at the top of the chat" />

The turns stay in the chat for you to read back; the model reads the summary
instead. Here the next turn went to the model at 7.8k tokens (**ctx** in its
header), where the one before the summary was 22k.

<Screenshot name="roleplay-after-summary" alt="The first turn after the summary, at a third of the size" />

`/compact(2)` keeps the last two turns as they are, under the summary. To
compact from an earlier turn, choose **Summarize up to here** from its **⋯**
menu.
