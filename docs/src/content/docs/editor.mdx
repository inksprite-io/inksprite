The editor formats Markdown as you type, and saves as you go.

## Tabs

Click a document in the outline to open it. It opens in a tab with its name in
italics: a preview, which the next document you click replaces. Type in it, or
double-click the document or its tab, to keep it open.

- Close a tab with its **×**, or click it with the middle button.
- Right-click a tab for **Copy link**, **Edit in plain text editor**,
  **Versions…** and **Keyboard shortcuts**.
- Each project remembers its tabs, and a tab you come back to is where you
  left it.

When the model changes an open document, the change shows at once, and
<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> on Windows and Linux)
undoes it like one of your own.

## Format as you type

Type the Markdown and it turns into what it stands for:

| Type | For |
| --- | --- |
| `#` and a space, up to `######` | A heading, level 1 to 6 |
| `-` or `*` and a space | A bulleted list |
| `1.` and a space | A numbered list |
| `>` and a space | A quote |
| ` ``` ` | A code block |
| `---` on a line of its own | A horizontal rule |
| `**bold**` | **bold** |
| `*italic*` | *italic* |
| `~~struck~~` | ~~struck~~ |
| `` `code` `` | `code` |

Press <kbd>Backspace</kbd> straight after to undo a conversion and keep what
you typed.

In a list, <kbd>Tab</kbd> indents an item and <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Tab</kbd>
outdents it. <kbd>Enter</kbd> on an empty item ends the list.
<kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> breaks a line without starting a new
paragraph, and takes you out of a code block.

### Quotes, dashes and ellipses

As in a word processor:

- `"` and `'` become curly quotes and apostrophes: “like this”.
- `--` becomes an em dash (—), except at the start of a line, so `---` on a
  line of its own is still a rule.
- `...` becomes an ellipsis (…).

None of this happens in code. To keep the straight characters, press
<kbd>Backspace</kbd> straight after.

## Format a selection

Select some words, and a bar comes up under them: **Comment**, **Link**,
**Bold** and **Italic**, then **⋯** for the rest, with **Clear formatting**,
which takes it all off. Its last button is the paragraph's style: **Normal
text**, a heading, a code block, or a quote or a list, which choosing again
takes off. On a phone, the bar is at the bottom of the screen.

## Tables

Type the header row, with a pipe at each end and between the columns, and
press <kbd>Enter</kbd>:

```
| Name | Role | First seen |
```

It becomes a table with an empty row under the header, and the caret in it.
Or right-click and choose **Insert** › **Table** for one of two columns.

- <kbd>Tab</kbd> goes to the next cell, and <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Tab</kbd>
  back.
- <kbd>Enter</kbd> goes down a row. From the last row, it leaves the table for
  a paragraph after it.
- <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> leaves the table from any cell.

A cell holds one line of text: several lines pasted into it are joined.

To add a row or a column, right-click a cell and point to **Table**. The same
menu deletes rows and columns, aligns a column's text, and has **Delete
table**.

<Screenshot name="editor-table-menu" alt="A table's menu, with Align column open" />

To delete a table from the keyboard, put the caret at the start of the line
after it. <kbd>Backspace</kbd> selects the table, again empties its cells, and
a third time deletes it.

## Links

Select some words and press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>
<kbd>K</kbd> on Windows and Linux). Type or paste the address and press
<kbd>Enter</kbd> or click the check. An address without `https://` gets it,
and an email address becomes a mail link. With nothing selected, the keys ask
for the link's **Text** too; with the caret in a link, they change both its
text and where it goes.

Paste an address that starts with `https://` over selected words to link them,
or on its own to put it in as a link.

Click in a link to see where it goes, with buttons beside it to copy, edit and
remove it. Right-click the link, or what it shows, for the same. Click the
address, or <kbd>⌘</kbd>-click the link (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>-click on Windows and
Linux), to follow it. In a browser it opens in a new tab; in the desktop app,
in your usual browser.

<Screenshot name="editor-link" alt="A link, with its address and buttons to copy, edit and remove it" />

### Link to another document

Type `[[` and the start of a document's name, and the documents it could be
are listed under it. Pick one with the arrow keys and <kbd>Enter</kbd>, or
click it, and a link to it goes in, the document's name as its words.
<kbd>Esc</kbd> puts the list away and leaves what you typed. The link field
lists documents the same way as you type in it.

You can also right-click a document in the outline, or its tab, choose **Copy
link**, and paste it: over selected words it links them, and on its own it
goes in with the document's name. Or drag the document from the outline into
the text.

The link is written the way other Markdown apps write it, from this document
to the other one: `../Notes/Cast%20list.md`. Click in it to see the
document's name, and click the name or <kbd>⌘</kbd>-click the link to open the
document in a tab.

Copied into a document in another folder, a link still goes to the same
document. Renaming or moving a document changes the links to it to match.

### Link to a heading

After a document's name, type `#` and the list shows its headings instead, as
in `[[Cast list#The crew`; `[[#` lists this document's own. The link opens the
document at that heading, and is written as Obsidian writes one:
`Cast%20list.md#The%20crew`. If the heading's words change, the link still
opens the document.

## Pictures

Paste a picture, drop one onto the page from your computer, or right-click and
choose **Insert** › **Picture…**. It goes in where the caret is. On a line of
its own, it's centred.

<Screenshot name="editor-picture" alt="A map in the document on a place, selected, with its alt text in a field under it" />

Click a picture to select it. The field under it is its alt text: a few words
on what it shows, which is all the model knows of the picture. Type them and
press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.

- Double-click a picture, or <kbd>⌘</kbd>-click it (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>-click on
  Windows and Linux), to see it over the whole window. Click it there for its
  own size, and press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to close it.
- Right-click a picture for **Alt text…**, **View picture** and **Save
  picture…**.
- A picture belongs to its document and isn't in the outline. Copied into
  another document, it's that document's own copy.
- Delete a picture as you would a word. **Undo** brings it back until you next
  open the project.
- A picture from a web address isn't loaded, so that the page you got it from
  can't see you open the document. It shows as a box naming the site.
- A Word file or a Google Doc you import keeps its pictures.

## Find and replace

With the caret in a document, press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd>
(<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd> on Windows and Linux), or click the magnifier at
the right end of the tabs. Words you've selected are what it looks for.

Type what to find. Every match is marked, and the bar says how many there are.
<kbd>Enter</kbd> goes to the next and <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> to the
one before. **Aa** (**Match case**) finds only text in the case you typed, and
**Whole words** stops *he* matching *the*.

To replace, click the arrow at the left of the bar and type the replacement.
**Replace** changes the match you're on and moves to the next; **All** changes
every one, as one step to undo. Replacements keep the formatting of what they
replace. With **Match case** off, a replacement typed in lower case takes the
capitals of each match: *she* over *He* is *She*.

<Screenshot name="editor-find" alt="Find and replace, with every match marked" />

<kbd>Esc</kbd> closes the bar, with the caret on the match you were on.

## The right-click menu

Right-click in a document for:

- **Cut**, **Copy** and **Paste**, as their keys do.
- **Copy as Markdown**, for an app that takes Markdown, such as GitHub.
- **Paste without formatting**, which keeps only the text.
- **Paste from Markdown**, which turns Markdown copied from elsewhere into
  headings, bold and the rest.
- **Insert link…**, or **Edit link…** and **Remove link** in a link.
- **Comment…** on selected text, or **Edit comment…** and **Resolve comment**
  in one: see [Comments](/comments/).
- **Insert**: a **Picture…** from your computer, at the caret; or a **Table**,
  a **Horizontal rule** or a **Code block**. These take an empty line's place,
  or go after the paragraph you're in.
- **Table**, in a table.

On a misspelled word, its corrections come first: see
[Spelling and grammar](#spelling-and-grammar). On a picture, its own items
come first: see [Pictures](#pictures). The menu key opens the menu at
the caret.

The first time you paste from the menu, your browser may ask to let inksprite
see what you copied. Safari asks every time. Shift-right-click opens your
browser's own menu instead.

## Spelling and grammar

inksprite checks your spelling and grammar on your own computer: nothing you
write is sent anywhere to be checked. A moment after you stop typing,
misspelled words are underlined in red and grammar in blue. A word you're
still typing isn't underlined until you move on from it, and nothing is
corrected for you.

Right-click an underlined word to see what it could be, and click one to put
it in place. For a misspelling:

- **Add to dictionary** stops the word being flagged in every project.
- **Add to project** stops it being flagged in this project.

On part of a hyphenated word, like the *Wi* in *Wi-Fi*, these add the whole
word, and say which.

The names in your documents' titles are known already: a character sheet
called *Marisol Vey* teaches inksprite both names.

For grammar, the menu says what's wrong. **Ignore** stops that one being
flagged where it is; change the words around it and it can come back.

Under **Spelling** in **Settings** › **System**:

- **Spell checker** turns inksprite's checker off, and leaves spelling to
  your browser, which checks other languages too. It starts off if your
  browser's language isn't English.
- **Language** is the English it checks: US, UK, Australia, Canada or India.
  It starts on your browser's.
- **Grammar** turns the blue underlines off, and leaves spelling.
- **Manage dictionary** lists the words you've added: **All projects** for
  every project, **This project** for the one that's open. Type a word and
  press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to add it, or click a word's **×** to take it out.

Only English is checked.

## The plain text editor

To see the Markdown underneath a document, or keep its text exactly as you
type it, right-click it in the outline, or its tab, and choose **Edit in plain
text editor**. Its tab is marked with **&lt;/&gt;**.

Choose it again, ticked now, to go back to the editor. If the editor had to
rewrite some of the Markdown to show it, it says so: **What changed?** shows
the lines, and **Undo** puts back what you typed, as plain text.

Find and replace works here too. A document imported from outside that's too
long for the editor to keep up with comes in as plain text.

## Export or copy a document

Right-click a document in the outline:

- **Export** saves it as a Markdown file (`.md`), without comments or the
  suggestions you haven't accepted. A document with pictures comes as a
  `.zip` of the Markdown file and an `images` folder beside it, which any
  Markdown app can open. A folder or the whole project exports the same way:
  see [Export](/projects/#export).
- **Duplicate** makes a copy beside it, named with **(2)**, and opens it: try
  something there without touching the original.

## Versions

inksprite keeps earlier versions of each document, to compare with the text
now and restore. Right-click the document in the outline, or its tab, and
choose **Versions…**.

A version is kept:

- when you change a document you'd left for 10 minutes or more: the text as
  you left it;
- every hour through a long stretch of writing;
- before anything else changes the text: the model's edits, a
  [conversion](/files/#convert-to-markdown) run again, a
  [Google Docs](/google-docs/) sync, edits [synced](/sync/) from another
  device, a card imported again, or restoring a version.

On the left are the versions, newest first and by day, each with its time,
what it was kept before, and its word count. Pick one to see it on the right as
the editor shows it, with what a restore would change marked: what it would
take out of the text now is struck through in red, and what it would bring
back is green. Copy from it, or:

- **Restore** puts the version in place of the text, and keeps the text it
  replaces as a version first, so a restore can be taken back.
- **Name**, the pencil, names the version. A named version is kept until you
  delete it; the bin (**Delete version**) deletes it.
- **Keep a version…** keeps the text as it is now, under a name.

Versions thin out as they age: every one from the last day, then one a day
for 30 days, then one a week. A document in the
[plain text editor](#the-plain-text-editor) shows its versions as written,
Markdown and all. Only text documents have versions, not imported files.

## Arrange the panels

- **Expand editor**, at the right end of the tabs, gives the editor the
  chat's room; click it again (**Show chat**) to bring the chat back.
  **Expand chat**, at the left of the chat's header, does the same the other
  way.
- Click the icon at the far left for the list that's showing (the outline,
  say) to hide the sidebar, and any of the icons to show it again.
- Drag the line between two panels to change their widths.

Each project remembers how you left it. In a narrow window, the sidebar and
the chat take turns beside the editor.

## Focus mode

Press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⌥</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Alt</kbd>
<kbd>F</kbd> on Windows and Linux), or click the four arrows at the right end
of the tabs, to see only the document you're writing. The sidebar, the chat
and the tabs go away, and the window goes full screen.

Press the same keys or <kbd>Esc</kbd> to come back.

## On a phone

The editor is the **Write** view, the pen at the top. The tabs show while a
document is open, and the magnifier at their right end opens find and replace.
Your keyboard checks your spelling, as it does everywhere else.
To put a picture in, paste it. The outline's menu, with **Export** and the
rest, is the **⋯** on each row.
