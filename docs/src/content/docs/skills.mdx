A skill is a piece of work with instructions of its own: summarizing a chat,
writing a scene, reviewing a draft. You call one by typing `/` and its name in
the chat. The model can be offered a skill too, and calls it when its
description fits what you asked.

## The built-in skills

- **Compact**, `/compact`, summarizes the chat to carry on from. Only you call
  it; see [Compact a long chat](/chats/#compact-a-long-chat).
- **Interpret**, `/interpret` and a question, asks for an idea: it draws a
  tarot card and reads it against the story, in your turn. It's made for games.
- **Write**, `/write` and what to write, writes prose from the project's notes,
  as the reply.

Interpret and Write are still being tuned, so they're listed under
**Experimental** in **Settings** › **Skills**, each with a switch, and each is
off until you switch it on there. Then you can type its command in any chat.
No built-in profile offers them to the model; to offer one in a chat, switch it
on under **Skills** in the [chat's settings](/chats/#skills).

## Call a skill

Type `/` at the start of a line in the message box, and the
[list](/chats/#commands) shows every command and skill. Keep typing to narrow
it, press <kbd>Tab</kbd> to finish the name, type what the skill needs after
it, and send. The sparkles mark skills that call the model; they take as long
as a reply does.

When the model calls a skill, its reply shows a block for it. Click the block
to see what the skill was asked, the tools it used, and what it answered.

## Make your own

Open **Settings** › **Skills** and click **New skill**. Hover a field's name
for a reminder of what it takes.

- **Name**: what you type after the `/`. Lowercase letters, digits and
  hyphens, and nothing the app already uses: a built-in skill, a command like
  `/roll`, or one of the model's tools.
- **Description**: what it does and when to use it. The model reads this to
  decide whether to call it.
- **Who can call it**: **You and the model**, **Only you**, as `/name`, or
  **Only the model**, when its description fits.
- **How it runs**:
  - **Joining the conversation**: its instructions go into the chat, and the
    model follows them there, with the chat's tools.
  - **On its own, reporting back**: it's a request of its own to the model. It
    reads the conversation so far, uses only the tools you give it, and
    answers.
- **Its answer**, for one that runs on its own:
  - **Back to whoever called it**: to the model, as what its call returned, or
    into your turn when you called it.
  - **As the reply**: what it writes is the reply.
- **Tools**, for one that runs on its own: the tools it may use, by the names
  the model knows them by, such as `read_document`.
- **Argument**: a name for what you type after the skill's name. Naming one
  makes it required.
- **Argument description**: what to type for it, such as *the document to
  review*. The `/` list shows it after the name, and the model is told it.
- **Instructions**: what the skill is told to do. `$ARGUMENTS`, or `$` and the
  argument's name, stands for what you typed; without either, it goes at the
  end.

Click **Save**. A skill belongs to the app, not a project, so its `/` command
works in every chat. Whether the model is offered it is up to each chat's
settings, under **Skills**; the Roleplay profiles start with your skills off.

### Example: a review skill

This skill reads a draft and critiques it as an editor would, as a reply of its
own.

1. In **Settings** › **Skills**, click **New skill**.
2. Fill in the form:
   - **Name**: `review`
   - **Description**: *Critique a draft the way an editor would: what works,
     where it drags or loses the reader, and the one change that would help
     most.*
   - **Who can call it**: **Only you, as /review**
   - **How it runs**: **On its own, reporting back**
   - **Its answer**: **As the reply**
   - **Tools**: `read_document` and `list_documents`
   - **Argument**: `document`
   - **Argument description**: *the document to review*
   - **Instructions**:

     ```md
     You are an editor reading a draft for its writer. Read $document with
     read_document, all of it, before you say anything.

     Then write a short critique:

     - What works, in two or three sentences.
     - Where the pace drags or the reader could get lost, quoting the lines you mean.
     - The one change that would help most.

     Don't rewrite the draft, and don't praise what doesn't earn it.
     ```
3. Click **Save**.

In a chat, type `/review` and the document's path. To get the path,
right-click the document in the outline and choose **Copy path**.

```
/review Manuscript/Prologue
```

The critique arrives as a turn of its own, headed **Review**.

<Screenshot name="skill-review-turn" alt="The review skill’s critique, as a turn of its own" />

To let the model call it as well, choose **You and the model** under **Who can
call it**. Then *what do you think of chapter 3?* can reach it, and the critique
is the model's reply.

### Saved prompts

A skill that joins the conversation, and that only you call, is a saved
prompt: instructions you'd otherwise type again and again. A new skill starts
as one. With these **Instructions**:

```md
Tighten $ARGUMENTS: cut every word that isn't working, keep the voice, and
show me the new version whole.
```

typing `/tighten the fight scene` puts the instructions into your turn, with
*the fight scene* in place of `$ARGUMENTS`, and the model answers them.

Let the model call it too, and the model loads the instructions into the chat
when its description fits, and follows them from then on. To stop it, click
**Drop** on the skill in the [chat's settings](/chats/#skills); it goes at the
next summary.

### The file

A skill is kept as a SKILL.md file, the format Claude, Codex and other tools
read. Click **File** at the top of the skill's editor to edit it as text, for
anything the form doesn't reach, and **Form** to go back. The review skill
above is:

```md
---
name: review
description: >-
  Critique a draft the way an editor would: what works, where it drags or loses
  the reader, and the one change that would help most.
disable-model-invocation: true
context: fork
arguments: document
argument-hint: the document to review
allowed-tools: read_document list_documents
---

You are an editor reading a draft for its writer. …
```

To delete a skill, open it and click **Delete**. Turns that used it keep what
it said.

## Import and export skills

Click **Import…** in **Settings** › **Skills** and choose **Files or a .zip…**
or **A folder…**. inksprite finds every SKILL.md in what you choose, and any
Markdown file with frontmatter of its own. Skills made for Claude or another
tool that reads SKILL.md files import as they are.

**Import skills** lists what it found. Switch off any you don't want, then
click **Import 1 skill**, or however many it says.

- **Replaces your…** means you have a skill by that name, and this one takes
  its place.
- A name the app already uses has to change before the skill can come in; type
  a new one in the field under it.
- A skill whose file doesn't read says what's wrong with it, and stays out.
- **Not used here** lists fields in the file that inksprite ignores, and **Left
  out** what it didn't bring, such as scripts.

**Export all** saves every skill of yours as a .zip, with a folder for each;
**Export** in a skill's editor saves that one. Either imports into inksprite,
or into another tool that reads skills.

## Reword a built-in skill

In **Settings** › **Skills**, click a skill under **Built-in** or
**Experimental** and change its **Instructions**. They save as you type, for
every chat, and the list marks the skill **reworded**. The reset button beside
**Instructions** puts back the wording it ships with.

A profile's own wording of a skill wins over yours in the chats on it. The
Roleplay profiles word **Compact** for games, and the editor says so under the
instructions.

### For one chat

To change how a skill runs in one chat, open the chat's settings, open the
skill under **Skills**, and edit its **Prompt**. This works for your own skills
as well as the built-ins. To keep the wording for new chats,
[save it to the profile](/chats/#save-changes-to-a-profile). The reset button
beside **Prompt** puts the skill back on its wording in **Settings** ›
**Skills**.
