A project holds one piece of work: its documents, the files you imported, and
its chats.

## Switch projects

The buttons at the top of the outline:

- **Projects**, the book, lists your projects by name, each with when it was
  last edited. Click one to open it where you left it.
- **Import a project**, the arrow, adds a project from a file: see
  [move a project](#move-a-project).
- **New project**, the plus, starts one.

A project with a name another already has gets a number: a second
*Lighthouse* is *Lighthouse (2)*.

## Project settings

Right-click the project, the first row of the outline, and choose **Project
settings**.

- **Title** is the project's name.
- **Summary** says what the project is. The model reads it with every message,
  so a few lines on the work and where it stands save explaining it in each
  chat.
- **Default profile** is the [profile](/chats/#profiles) new chats here start
  on. Chats you've already started keep theirs.

Click **Save**.

## Arrange the outline

Right-click a row for its menu, or the space below the last row for the
project's. On a phone, press and hold a row, or tap its **⋯**.

### Order

A folder lists its folders first, then its documents, each by name, with
numbers in order: *Chapter 2* comes before *Chapter 10*. Choose **Keep in
order** from a folder's menu to keep them in the order you drag them into, with
new ones at the end. **Sort by name** goes back to sorting by name, and
remembers your order for when you choose **Keep in order** again.

### Move

Drag a document or a folder onto a folder to put it inside; a closed folder
opens if you hold it there. Drop it between two rows to put it in their
folder. In a folder kept in order, it stays where you drop it.

### Names

No two documents in a folder share a name, whatever their capitals. A name you
type that's taken is refused, and the outline says what has it. Something
moved, imported or copied into a folder that has its name gets a number:
*Notes (2)*.

### The row's menu

- **Rename** edits the name; so does <kbd>F2</kbd>.
- **Duplicate** copies a document beside itself and opens the copy.
- **Download** saves a document as a Markdown file, or an imported file as it
  came in.
- **Copy path** copies where the document is, such as `Notes/Characters/Elara`,
  for naming it in a chat.
- **Hide from all chats** keeps it out of every chat's sight. Pins and hiding
  in one chat are in [Chats](/chats/#what-the-model-sees-of-your-project).
- **Import**, on a folder, brings files in: see
  [PDFs, Images, and Ebooks](/files/).

From the keyboard, the arrow keys move along the outline and open and close
folders, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens a document.

### Delete

Choose **Delete** from the row's menu. A folder goes with everything in it. To
delete a whole project and its chats, choose **Delete project** from the
project's menu.

There's no undo: if you might want it back, [back up](#back-up-everything)
first. In the desktop app, with
[Keep projects in a folder](/desktop/#keep-projects-in-a-folder) on, what you
delete goes to your computer's trash.

## Move a project

A project file carries one project to another browser, to the desktop app, or
to someone else.

1. Right-click the project and choose **Export project**. inksprite saves a
   file named after it, with its documents, imported files and chats, and any
   profiles of yours its chats use.
2. Where you want it, click **Import a project** at the top of the outline
   (**Import project** when no project is open) and choose the file.

It comes in as a new project beside the others, and changes nothing that's
there. Your settings and keys stay behind.

## Move your settings

A settings file carries your setup without your projects: your AI connections,
presets, chat profiles, MCP servers, skills, web search, and the choices you
made in **Settings**. Use one to set up another browser or the desktop app, or
to share your setup with someone.

1. Open **Settings** › **System**, click **Export** and choose **Settings**.
2. Untick anything you don't want in it. Ticking a preset ticks the connection
   it runs on. Your preferences (theme, how edits are applied, NSFW profiles
   and speaker colours) stay out unless you tick them.
3. Leave **Include API keys** off unless the file stays with you, and click
   **Export**.

To bring one in, click **Import** and choose the file. inksprite says
what the file adds and what it changes, and asks before it goes on, then
reloads. Nothing else changes: anything you have that the file doesn't is
kept, a key the file left out keeps yours, and your projects aren't touched.
If the file moves a connection or an MCP server to another address, your key
for it is left out, so you'd add it again. An MCP server you already have
keeps the tools you allowed; one the file adds asks before it runs a tool, as
any new server does.

## Back up everything

A backup holds every project, chat and setting.

1. Open **Settings** › **System** and click **Export**. Leave **Everything**
   chosen.
2. Leave **Include API keys** off unless the file stays with you. It covers the
   keys for your AI providers, MCP servers and web search.
3. Click **Export**. inksprite saves a file named with the date.

To restore one, click **Import** and choose the file. inksprite says
what the backup holds and asks before it goes on: **Replace** deletes
everything inksprite holds now, puts the backup in its place, and reloads.

A backup made without keys comes back without them. Before you restore it,
inksprite lists the services whose keys were left out, so you know which to
connect again. Your sign-ins to [MCP servers](/mcp/) and the **Debug** switch
are never in a backup. A backup from an older version of inksprite restores
too.

## Where your work is kept

In a browser, your work is in that browser's storage, on that computer. Another
browser starts empty: bring your work over with a backup or a project file.
Clearing the site's data deletes it.

When you start a project, inksprite asks the browser to keep its data. Firefox
asks you first; allow it.

:::caution[Safari deletes it after a week]
Safari deletes a site's data after a week without a visit. On an iPhone or an
iPad, add inksprite to your
[Home Screen](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/bookmark-a-website-iph42ab2f3a7/ios#iph4f9a47bbc)
to keep it. On a Mac, use the desktop app, or back up often.
:::

The [desktop app](/desktop/) keeps your work where no browser can clear it,
and can keep each project as a folder of Markdown files.
