A project holds one piece of work: its documents, the files you imported, and
its chats.

## Switch projects

Click **Projects**, the menu at the top left of the outline. **Switch project**
lists your projects by name, each with when it was last edited: click one to
open it where you left it. **New project…** starts one, and
**Import project…** adds one from a file: see [move a project](#move-a-project).
**Project settings**, **Export project** and **Delete project** are for the
project that's open. **Trash** is there while anything is in the
[trash](#delete-and-the-trash). Signed in to [sync](/sync/), **Keep on this
device only…** keeps the project off it.

A project with a name another already has gets a number: a second
*Thief* is *Thief (2)*.

## Project settings

Choose **Project settings** from **Projects**, or right-click the project, the
first row of the outline, and choose it there. It opens **Settings** ›
**Project**, which is the open project's own.

- **Title** is the project's name.
- **Summary** says what the project is. The model reads it with every message,
  so a few lines on the work and where it stands save explaining it in each
  chat.
- **Default chat profile** is the [profile](/chats/#profiles) new chats here
  start on. Chats you've already started keep theirs.
- **Sharing**, while you're signed in to [sync](/sync/), is who has the
  project: see [share a project](/sync/#share-a-project).

Each is kept as you change it.

## Arrange the outline

Right-click a row for its menu, or the space below the last row for the
project's. On a phone, press and hold a row, or tap its **⋯**.

The plus at the top of the outline starts a document at the top of the
project, and **Import** beside it brings files in there: see
[PDFs, Images, and Ebooks](/files/).

### Order

A folder lists its folders first, then its documents, each by name, with
numbers in order: *Chapter 2* comes before *Chapter 10*. Choose **Keep in
order** from a folder's menu to keep them in the order you drag them into, with
new ones at the end. **Sort by name** goes back to sorting by name, and
remembers your order for when you choose **Keep in order** again.

### Move

Drag a document or a folder onto a folder to put it inside; a closed folder
opens if you hold it there. Drop it between two rows to put it in their
folder. The folder it would go into is shaded while you drag. In a folder kept
in order, it stays where you drop it.

### Documents that hold others

Some documents hold others, as a folder does: a book you import holds its
chapters, a character card its parts, a Scrivener text the scenes under it, and
a file the Markdown [converted](/files/#convert-to-markdown) from it. Click the
arrow beside one to see what it holds, and the document itself to open it.
What's in it opens, moves and renames like any document, and can be dragged
out, but nothing can be dragged in. Hiding or deleting the document hides or
deletes what it holds with it.

### Names

No two documents in a folder share a name, whatever their capitals. A name you
type that's taken is refused, and the outline says what has it. Something
moved, imported or copied into a folder that has its name gets a number:
*Notes (2)*.

### The row's menu

- **Rename** edits the name; so does <kbd>F2</kbd>, or clicking the name of
  the document that's open.
- **Duplicate** copies a document beside itself and opens the copy.
- **Versions…** shows a document's earlier versions, to compare and restore:
  see [Versions](/editor/#versions).
- **Export** saves a document as a Markdown file, or with its pictures as a
  `.zip` of the file and an `images` folder. **Download** saves an imported
  file as it came in.
- **Copy path** copies where the document is, such as `Notes/Characters/Elara`,
  for naming it in a chat.
- **Hide from all chats** keeps it out of every chat's sight. Pins and hiding
  in one chat are in [Chats](/chats/#what-the-model-sees-of-your-project).
- **Import**, on a folder, brings files in: see
  [PDFs, Images, and Ebooks](/files/).
- **Explore…**, on a folder or the project, has the model write a line on
  what each document is about, for the chats to find their way by: see
  [Chats](/chats/#explore-the-project).

From the keyboard, the arrow keys move along the outline and open and close
folders, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens a document.

### Undo

<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> on Windows and Linux)
in the outline takes back the last rename, move, delete or new document, and
<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd>
<kbd>Z</kbd>) does it again. Taking back a new document moves it, and
whatever you wrote in it, to the trash. In the editor the same keys
undo your typing.

## Delete and the trash

Choose **Delete** from the row's menu. A folder goes with everything in it. To
delete a whole project and its chats, choose **Delete project** from
**Projects** or the project's menu. A chat is deleted from its menu in the
[chat list](/chats/#the-chat-list).

Nothing asks first: what you delete goes to the trash, and is kept there for
30 days, then deleted for good. A document goes with its comments and
versions, and an imported file with the file itself. What the model deletes
goes there too. <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> in the outline takes back a delete
you made there.

To see what's in it, choose **Trash** from **Projects**. It lists the open
project's documents, folders and chats, newest first, each with where it was
and when it went, then the projects in the trash. With no project open,
**Trash**, beside **New project** and **Import project**, lists the projects
in the trash.

- The circular arrow (**Restore**) puts it back where it was, or at the top of
  the project if its folder has gone. A name taken since gets a number.
- The cross (**Delete now**) deletes it for good, after asking.
- **Empty trash** deletes everything in it for good, after asking.

A turn you delete in a chat doesn't go to the trash, nor does a card you
delete from the
[card library](/roleplay/#keep-your-cards-in-the-library). In the desktop app,
what's deleted for good goes to your computer's trash.

## Move a project

A project file carries one project to another browser, to the desktop app, or
to someone else.

1. Choose **Export project** from **Projects**, or right-click the project.
   inksprite saves a file named after it, with its documents, imported files
   and chats, and any profiles of yours its chats use.
2. Where you want it, choose **Import project…** from **Projects** at the top
   of the outline (click **Import project** when no project is open), and
   choose the file.

It comes in as a new project beside the others, and changes nothing that's
there. Your settings and keys stay behind, and so do the project's trash and
its documents' [versions](/editor/#versions).

## Bring in a Scrivener project

Choose **Import project…** from **Projects**, and choose the `.scriv`, or a
zip of it.

It comes in as a new project named after it, with the binder as the outline:
the same folders in the same order, each text a document, and the PDFs and
pictures in Research as files. A text with others under it in the binder holds
them here too. A text's synopsis and notes are documents of their own, after
it, or first among what it holds. To bring one into a project that's open, choose it with
**Import › Files…** on a folder, or drop it there.

Headings, lists, bold and italic, and tables come with the text; fonts,
colors and spacing don't, nor do labels, keywords, snapshots or compile
settings. Comments, footnotes and pictures in the text aren't handled yet. A
project from before Scrivener 3 needs opening in Scrivener 3 first. If a
browser can't read the `.scriv`, compress it in Finder and import the zip.

## Move your settings

A settings file carries your setup without your projects: your AI connections,
presets, chat profiles, MCP servers, skills, web search, and the choices you
made in **Settings**. Use one to set up another browser or the desktop app, or
to share your setup with someone.

1. Open **Settings** › **Sync and sharing** › **Backup**, click **Export**, and untick
   **Projects** and **Character cards**.
2. Open **Settings** and untick anything you don't want in the file. Ticking a
   preset ticks the connection it runs on.
3. Leave **Include API keys** off unless the file stays with you, and click
   **Export**.

To bring one in, click **Import** and choose the file. inksprite says
what the file adds and what it changes, and asks before it goes on, then
reloads. Nothing else changes: anything you have that the file doesn't is
kept, a key the file left out keeps yours, and your projects aren't touched.
If the file moves a connection or an MCP server to another address, your key
for it is left out, so you'd add it again. An MCP server you already have
keeps the tools you allowed; one the file adds asks before it runs a tool, as
any new server does.

## Export part of your work

**Export** lists your projects, character cards and settings, all ticked.
Untick what you want to leave out. Open a project to pick its documents and
chats; a document brings the folders above it.

To bring the file in, click **Import** and choose it. Its projects come in as
new ones beside yours, and its character cards join your library unless you
have them already. If it also changes settings, inksprite says what it adds and
changes, and asks before it goes on; its settings are merged as a settings
file's are.

## Back up everything

A backup holds every project, chat and setting, with what's in the trash and
every document's versions.

1. Open **Settings** › **Sync and sharing** › **Backup** and click **Export**. Leave
   everything ticked.
2. Leave **Include API keys** off unless the file stays with you. It covers the
   keys for your AI providers, MCP servers and web search.
3. Click **Export**. inksprite saves a file named with the date.

To restore one, click **Import** and choose the file. inksprite says
what the backup holds and asks before it goes on: **Replace** deletes
everything inksprite holds now, puts the backup in its place, and reloads.
Restoring signs the device out of [sync](/sync/).

A backup made without keys comes back without them. Before you restore it,
inksprite lists the services whose keys were left out, so you know which to
connect again. Your sign-ins to [MCP servers](/mcp/) and the **Debug** switch
are never in a backup. A backup from an older version of inksprite restores
too.

## Where your work is kept

In a browser, your work is in that browser's storage, on that computer. Another
browser starts empty: bring your work over with a backup or a project file, or
[sync](/sync/) it. Clearing the site's data deletes it, though a synced project
comes back when you sign in again.

When you start a project, inksprite asks the browser to keep its data. Firefox
asks you first; allow it.

:::caution[Safari deletes it after a week]
Safari deletes a site's data after a week without a visit. On an iPhone or an
iPad, [add inksprite to your Home Screen](/getting-started/#install-it-from-your-browser)
to keep it, and bring your projects over with a backup or [sync](/sync/), since
the Home Screen app starts empty. On a Mac, use the desktop app, or back up
often.
:::

The [desktop app](/desktop/) keeps your work where no browser can clear it,
and can keep each project as a folder of Markdown files.
