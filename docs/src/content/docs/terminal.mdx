A terminal is your own shell in a tab among your chats, for anything you'd
run in a terminal app: a build, a script, a coding agent.

It works only in the [desktop app](/desktop/), and while **Enable AI** is on
in **Settings** › **System**: its tab is among the chats.

## Turn it on

The terminal is an extension, off until you turn it on.

Open **Settings** › **Extensions** and switch on **Terminal**.

## Open a terminal

Click **New terminal**, beside **New chat** at the top of the chat. It opens
in a tab of its own, in your home folder, running the shell you use in any
other terminal (PowerShell on Windows).

Its tab is named by the program running in it, and **Terminal** until one
names it. Drag it, switch to it and close it as you would a chat's.

## A terminal belongs to its project

Open another project and a terminal keeps running. Come back, and its tab is
where you left it, with everything printed since.

A dot on its tab means a program in it rang the bell while you were looking
at something else, as a coding agent does when it needs you.

A terminal ends when:

- You close its tab. Nothing asks first, even with something running.
- Its shell ends, as on `exit`. Its tab closes.
- Its project goes: to the trash, or because the person who shared it with
  you stopped.
- You quit inksprite. Terminals aren't there when you open it again.

## What a terminal can do

A terminal is your shell, and what you run in it can do anything you can do on
your computer. It isn't fenced the way [Python](/python/) is. The model has no
part in it: no chat can type in a terminal or read one.

What a terminal shows is kept only while it runs, on this computer. It isn't
saved with the project, and it isn't [synced](/sync/).

On a Mac, the first time a program in a terminal opens your Documents, Desktop
or Downloads folder, macOS asks whether inksprite may. A yes there is for
inksprite as a whole.

## Keys

What you type goes to the shell, keys with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> included. The
terminal keeps these for itself:

| Action | Mac | Windows and Linux |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copy | <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>C</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>C</kbd> |
| Paste | <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>V</kbd> | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>V</kbd> |
| Select all | <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> | |
| Clear | <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> | |
| A line break where <kbd>Enter</kbd> sends | <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> | <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> |
| Open a link | <kbd>⌘</kbd>-click | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>-click |

Point at a link and where it leads shows at the foot of the terminal. It opens
in your usual browser, and an email address in your mail app.

On a Mac, [inksprite's own shortcuts](/keyboard-shortcuts/#in-the-desktop-app)
work from a terminal: <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>W</kbd> closes its tab. On Windows and
Linux they're on <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>, which goes to the shell while you're in a
terminal, so click in the editor or a chat first.
