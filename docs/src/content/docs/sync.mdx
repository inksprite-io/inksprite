title: Sync Between Devices
description: Keep your projects and setup on every device you use, and share a project with friends, encrypted so that the server can't read them.
---

:::note[Private preview]
Sync is open to a few testers for now, by invitation.
:::

Sync keeps your work on every device you sign in on: the desktop app on two
computers, say, and a browser on your phone. Each device keeps a whole copy and
works offline, and the sync server at `sync.inksprite.io` passes the changes
between them. Everything is encrypted on your device before it's sent, so the
server can't read it.

Sync is optional. Without it, your work stays on the device it's on. With it,
you can also [share a project](#share-a-project) with friends who have
accounts.

## Make an account

For now, an account takes an invitation. Open the link in it, and inksprite
opens **Settings** › **Sync and sharing** on **Create account**, with the
**Sign-up code** filled in. The desktop app doesn't open the link: choose
**Create account** there, and type the code, the part of the link after
`invite=`.

1. Type a **Name**. It's 3 to 30 of the letters a to z, digits and hyphens,
   with each hyphen between two of the others: `sam-k`, not `-sam` or
   `sam--k`. Capitals don't make it another name.
2. Type a **Passphrase** of 12 characters or more.
3. Click **Create account**.

inksprite shows your **Recovery code**. If you forget the passphrase, it's the
only way back into the account: no one can reset a passphrase, us included.
Click **Copy**, keep the code where you keep the passphrase, and click
**Done**. It isn't shown again.

The device then sends up everything it has.

## Sign in on another device

Open **Settings** › **Sync and sharing** there, type your **Name** and
**Passphrase**, and click **Sign in**. Each device asks for the passphrase
once.

The device takes your projects and setup from the account, and adds what only
it had: a project made there goes to your other devices too. An imported file,
such as a PDF, comes down the first time you open it, so a phone doesn't fetch
every one at once. Its text is there from the start, for you and the model to
search. Opened offline before then, it says **Not on this device yet**, with
**Try again**.

## What syncs

- Every project: its documents and folders, imported files, chats, the trash,
  and each document's [versions](/editor/#versions).
- Your setup: your AI connections with their keys, presets, chat profiles,
  skills, MCP servers and web search, your
  [card library](/roleplay/#keep-your-cards-in-the-library), and the choices
  in **Settings** that a [settings file](/projects/#move-your-settings)
  carries, your dictionary among them.

What stays with each device:

- Sign-ins to [MCP servers](/mcp/) and [Google Drive](/google-docs/): sign in
  on each device.
- A few settings that belong to the device, such as **Debug** and whether the
  spell checker is on.
- Where you left each project: its tabs and panels.
- [Narration](/narration/) audio, which can be made again.
- A job under way, such as a [conversion](/files/#convert-to-markdown). It
  runs on the device that started it, and what it makes syncs.

## While you work

A change goes to your other devices a few seconds after you make it, and shows
there even in a document that's open. Offline, changes wait on the device, and
go when it's back online.

Edits to one document on two devices come together: edits to different
paragraphs, or to different words of one, both stand. Where both changed the
same words, the device that synced last wins, and the other words are kept as a
[version](/editor/#versions) of the document, to restore or copy from.

A delete on one device goes to the [trash](/projects/#delete-and-the-trash) on
all of them, and can be restored from any.

In **Settings** › **Sync and sharing**, a line says what's happening: **Up to date**,
**Syncing…**, or what went wrong. The arrows beside it (**Sync now**) sync at
once.

If another device, or someone you share a project with, has synced from a
newer version of inksprite, this one says *Some changes can't sync until
inksprite is updated*, with **Update** once the update is ready. What came from
the newer version waits on the server until you update, and your own changes
to anything it touched wait on this device.

## Keep a project on this device only

While you're signed in, choose **Keep on this device only…** from
**Projects**, or from the project's menu, and confirm. The project leaves the
sync server, and your other devices move it to their trash. Its files that are
only on the server are downloaded first.

**Sync this project**, in the same place, sends it up again.
**Settings** › **Sync and sharing** names the projects you keep this way.

## Share a project

You can share a project with friends who have accounts too. Everyone you share
it with can write in it, comment, and change its documents and folders, and
their changes reach you as yours reach them. Chats aren't shared: each
person's chats in the project stay theirs, on their own devices.

Before you share, update inksprite on every device you sync. One that hasn't
been updated can't sync a shared project, and says it waits for an update.

### Your friend code

In **Settings** › **Sync and sharing** › **Account**, click the copy button
beside your **Friend code**, and send the code to whoever wants to share a
project with you. The people you share with know you by your account's name.

Your friend code doesn't sign in to anything. It only tells their device who
you are, and lets it check that the server hasn't swapped in someone else.
Whoever has it can invite you to a project, but nothing of the project reaches
you unless you accept.

### Share yours

1. Choose **Share…** from **Projects**, or from the project's menu. It opens
   **Settings** › **Project** › **Sharing**.
2. Under **Share with**, paste your friend's friend code, or start typing the
   name of someone you've shared with before and pick them. Click **Share**.

Your friend is invited, and the project appears in their projects once they
accept. **Sharing** lists who has it, each by name and friend code, with
*Invited* beside anyone who hasn't answered yet. The cross beside someone
takes them out, or takes the invitation back. A removed friend keeps what they
already had, in their trash. You aren't told whether someone accepts or
rejects, only that they're no longer *Invited*.

Sharing first sends your chats in the project again, encrypted so that only you
can read them. If a reply is still being written in one of them, let it finish
and try again.

A shared project counts toward your account's storage, with everything your
friends add to it.

### A project shared with you

When someone shares a project with you, a notice says who and which project,
with their friend code. Check the code against the one they gave you before
you accept.

- **Accept** brings the project in, like your own.
- **Reject** turns it down. They can share it again.
- **Block** turns it down and blocks them (see below).

If you close the notice, the invitation waits at the top of **Projects…**,
greyed, with the same answers on its row, and a dot on the **Projects** menu
says it's there. An invitation you don't answer goes after 30 days.

Once you've accepted it, the project's menu has **People…**, which shows who
has it in **Settings** › **Project** › **Sharing**. **Projects…** says whose
each project is and who else is in it.

### Removing a shared project

**Remove** moves a shared project to your trash, the same as one of your own,
and **Undo** in the notice brings it back. It stays in everyone else's
projects. While it's in your trash it doesn't sync, so nothing of it downloads;
restore it and it catches up.

After 30 days in your trash, or once you delete it for good, you leave it. If
it's yours, it passes to the person who joined it first, and counts toward
their storage from then on. Your chats in it, and any documents you kept from
them, go with you. With no one left to take it, it's deleted.

### People and blocking

**Settings** › **Sync and sharing** › **People** lists everyone you've shared
with, and everyone whose project you've accepted, so you can share with them
by name. To add someone before you share anything, click the plus beside
**People** and paste their friend code. Right-click someone, or press and hold
on a phone, to copy their friend code, forget them (once nothing is shared
between you), or block them.

Block someone from their invitation or from **People**. You stop sharing with
each other: they're taken out of your projects, and you leave theirs. If any
project is shared between you, inksprite asks first. Their projects come back
to you as your own, kept on this device, unless you tick **Delete their
projects**, which moves them to your trash. Their invitations no longer reach
you, and they aren't told they're blocked. The people you've blocked are only
listed once you click the blocked button beside **People**, where their menu
has **Unblock**. What the block ended stays ended.

### Working together

Comments show who wrote them, by each person's account name, and you can only
edit your own. When two of you change the same words, the one who synced last
wins, and the other's words are kept as a [version](/editor/#versions) of the
document, as between your own devices.

## Your account

In **Settings** › **Sync and sharing**, once you're signed in:

- Beside what sync is doing, a line says how much the account holds, as in
  *120 MB of 2 GB used*. An
  account has 2 GB unless its invitation says otherwise, and imported files
  are most of what it holds. When it's full, the status line says so, and
  changes that don't fit wait on the device.
- Under **Account**, your [friend code](#your-friend-code) comes first, then
  **Send diagnostics**, which sends the server counts of what sync did on this
  device, such as how many changes went each way and how many rounds failed.
  Never your text, titles or names. See [About](/about/#sync).
- **Make a recovery code…**, or **New recovery code…**, asks for your
  passphrase and shows a new code. The old one stops working.
- **Change passphrase…** asks for the passphrase you have and a new one. Your
  other devices stay signed in.
- **Sign out** signs this device out. Its projects stay on it.
- **People** is [the people you share with](#people-and-blocking).
- **Devices** lists the devices signed in, this one first. The arrow beside
  another (**Sign out**) signs it out. What it has stays on it, keys included,
  so if it's lost, change your AI providers' keys too.

Restoring a [backup](/projects/#back-up-everything) with **Replace** signs the
device out too. Sign in again, and what the backup brought syncs as it would
from a new device.

## Forgot your passphrase

Choose **Recover**, type your **Name**, your **Recovery code** and a **New
passphrase**, and click **Recover account**. inksprite shows a new recovery
code, since each one works once.

Without the passphrase or the recovery code, no one can open the account. Your
devices still have everything on them; only the copy on the server is lost.

## What the server sees

Your passphrase never leaves your device. The keys made from it encrypt your
projects and setup, so the server keeps them but can't read them. It does see
your account's name, what your devices call themselves (such as *Safari on
iPhone*), how many records you sync and how large they are, when they change,
and the addresses your devices connect from.

Your keys sync too, encrypted like the rest. So someone who has your
passphrase has your AI providers' keys as well as your writing: use a
passphrase you don't use anywhere else.

If you share a project, the people you share it with can read it, but the
server still can't. It does see who each project is shared with, and who is
among your people and who you've blocked. An invitation's project name is
encrypted for the person invited. Your friend
code is made from a key your devices create for sharing. The server keeps its
public half for others to find you by, and its private half encrypted like the
rest.
