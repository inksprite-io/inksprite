---
title: Google Docs
description: Import Google Docs from your Drive into a project as Markdown documents you can edit, sync them with their Docs, put a document in a new Doc, and bring in other files you keep there.
---

Bring a draft you started in Google Docs into a project. Each Doc becomes a
document of its own, with its headings, lists and tables, ready to edit and
for the model to read. Sync it, and your edits go to the Doc and the Doc's
come back.

## Import from Drive

1. Right-click the project, or the folder to put the Docs in, and choose
   **Import** › **Google Drive…**.
2. Google's sign-in opens in a window of its own (in the desktop app, in your
   usual browser). Choose your Google account and allow inksprite to see the
   files you choose.
3. Google's file picker opens. Choose the Docs or other files to import; you
   can choose several at once.

inksprite puts them in the folder, and a message names any it couldn't
import. In the desktop app, the browser tab says you can
close it and go back to inksprite.

Google's picker opens each time you import, and inksprite can open only the
files you choose.

:::tip
If your browser blocks Google's window, inksprite says so. Click
**Try again**. If you close Google's window without choosing anything, click
**Cancel** in inksprite.
:::

Google's picker lets you open folders to find files, but choose the files
themselves: a folder chosen whole is skipped.

## What comes across

- **Google Docs** and **Word files** (`.docx`) become text documents, in
  Markdown: headings, lists, bold and italic, links, tables, horizontal
  lines and pictures. A drawing, or a picture in a table, is left out, and
  the message says how many.
- A Doc's **footnotes** come in at its end. Its **smart chips** come in as
  words: a person as their name, linked to their email address, and a date
  as it shows.
- A Doc with **suggested edits** still open comes in as it was before them,
  and the message names it. Its first sync brings them in as
  [suggestions](#suggested-edits).
- **Google Sheets** come as a CSV file of the first sheet.
- **Google Slides** come as a PDF, and **Google Drawings** as a PNG image.
- **Other files**, such as PDFs, images and Markdown files, import as they
  would from your computer. See [PDFs, Images, and Ebooks](/files/).
- **Forms**, **Sites** and other Google files that can't be exported are
  skipped, and so is a Doc over Drive's 10 MB export limit.

Files on shared drives import too.

## Sync with the Doc

An imported Google Doc stays linked to its document. Sync it to send your
edits to the Doc and bring in the ones made there, by you or anyone you share
it with.

1. Click the sync button at the end of the editor's tabs, or right-click the
   document and choose **Google Doc** › **Sync**.
2. If inksprite isn't signed in to Google, Google's sign-in opens for the
   account the Doc came from, as it does for an import.

The sync button spins until it's done. Edits on both sides come together, and
in an open document **Undo** takes back what the sync changed, in one step. The
text from before the sync is kept as a [version](/editor/#versions) too. Where
both sides changed the same words, this document's words win, and the Doc's
text from before the sync is kept as a version, to compare and restore. Google
Docs keeps it in its own version history too.

A document imported before inksprite could sync, or brought back from a
backup, doesn't know how the Doc was when they last agreed. Its first sync puts
this document in the Doc as it is, and keeps the Doc's text as a version. A Doc
with several tabs imported then can't sync: import it again. A Word file from
Drive isn't linked.

Some changes can't go to the Doc yet: footnotes added or taken out, a list
item's new kind or nesting, a heading inside a list, and rows or columns of a
table with merged cells. Sync says which and sends nothing; change it back, or
make the change in Google Docs. If the Doc has a new paragraph, a paragraph
parted or joined, or a change of formatting suggested where the document
changed, accept or reject it first, here or in Google Docs. Pictures you add
here stay here, and pictures added in the Doc come in.

### Comments

Comments go both ways too. A comment you or the assistant make here shows
in Google Docs on the same words, and a comment someone makes in the Doc
shows here, under their name. Replies, edits to your own comments, and
resolving or reopening a comment go across on the next sync, whichever side
they were made on. A comment someone deletes in the Doc is resolved here.

A comment whose words are gone here comes in without a place, in the
Comments tab. A comment longer than Google Docs allows stays here. In Google
Docs, everything inksprite sends is in your name, the assistant's comments too.

### Suggested edits

Edits suggested in the Doc come in as [suggestions](/comments/#suggestions),
under the name of whoever suggested them, with the replies on them. Accept or
reject one here, and the next sync accepts or rejects it in the Doc; one
settled in the Doc is settled here. Replies go both ways, as a comment's do.

Change the words of a suggestion from the Doc, and the next sync changes it
in the Doc too, still under its author's name. A suggestion of one letter
can't be changed in the Doc, so the words you typed go in beside it as a
suggestion of yours. Now and then sync can't send your change, such as
words typed into a paragraph parted in two: it stops and asks you to accept
or reject the suggestion first, so your change isn't lost.

The model's [suggestions](/comments/#suggestions) go to the Doc as suggested
edits in your name, as its comments do, with its notes on them as replies,
and come back as they were. One that changes formatting, or adds, parts or
joins a paragraph, stays here until you accept or reject it.

A suggestion that only changes formatting, such as making words bold, stays
in the Doc, and so does one on words you've since changed here. Now and then
suggestions that change one paragraph can't all show here, such as one that
parts the paragraph in two beside others that change its words: the one left
out comes in once the others are settled.

The **Google Doc** submenu also has **Open in Google Docs**, and **Unlink…**,
which leaves the Doc as it is. To link it again, import the Doc again: it
arrives beside the first as **Name (2)**. Or sync the document to a new Doc.

## Sync to a new Doc

To start a Doc from a document written here, right-click the document and
choose **Sync to a new Google Doc**. inksprite makes a Google Doc with the
document's name, puts the document in it, and links the two, so it syncs from
then on as above.

The Doc goes in a folder called **inksprite-sync** in your Drive, in a folder
for the project and one for each folder the document is in here. Rename or
move the document, its folders or the project, and the next sync renames or
moves them in Drive too. You can move or rename **inksprite-sync** itself.
Docs you imported stay where you keep them.

The Doc goes in the Google account the project last synced with. If the
project hasn't synced yet, Google's sign-in asks which account to use.

If the document has something a Doc can't take yet, such as footnotes or a
list inside a list, inksprite says so and makes no Doc. Pictures stay here.

In a browser, a sign-in lasts an hour, and syncs within it don't ask again.
The desktop app stays signed in until you take its access away in your Google
account.

:::note
**Google Drive…** is on the menu at [inksprite.io](https://inksprite.io) and in
the [desktop app](/desktop/). A copy of inksprite you run yourself has it only
if it's set up with a Google project of its own.
:::
