In the chat you work through your project with a model, which reads, searches
and writes its documents when you ask. Each project keeps its own chats.

## Start a chat

Type in the box at the bottom of the chat and press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to send;

<kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> starts a new line. On a phone, Return starts a
new line and the send arrow sends. A message you haven't sent stays in the box,
even through a reload.

- **New chat**, the **+** at the top of the chat, opens a fresh one in a
  [tab](#tabs) of its own. The model names it from your first message.
- <kbd>Esc</kbd>, or the stop button, stops a reply. What it wrote stays. You
  can write your next message while it replies, and send it once it's done.
- To add a turn without asking for a reply, right-click send, or hold a finger
  on it, and choose **Add as user** or **Add as assistant**, or press
  <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>⌥</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> (
  <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>
  <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Alt</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> on Windows and Linux).
  Write the model's side yourself to show it what you want.

Math written in TeX, between `$…$` in a sentence or `$$…$$` on lines of its
own, is typeset, in your messages and the model's. Copying a formula copies its
TeX.

## The chat list

Click the **Chats** icon at the far left. The most recent chat is first, and
**Search chats…** searches titles and messages. On a phone, the arrow at the
top left of a chat goes back to the list.

Click **⋮** on a chat, or right-click it, to **Rename**, **Export** or
**Delete** it. A deleted chat goes to the
[trash](/projects/#delete-and-the-trash). **Import a chat**, the upload arrow
at the top of the list, brings an exported chat into the open project. It
takes SillyTavern chats too, and files each under its character's card when
the card is in the project.

## Tabs

Each chat you open has a tab above it, as documents have above the editor.

- A chat you click in the chat list opens in a tab in italics, which the next
  chat you click takes over. Send something in it, or double-click its tab, to
  keep it.
- **New chat** and **Fork from here** open a tab beside the one you're in.
- Close a tab with its **×**, or click it with the middle button, and you're
  back in the tab you were in before it. Closing the last leaves a new chat.
- Right-click a tab to **Close others**, **Export** or **Delete** its chat.
- A spinner on a tab, and on the chat in the chat list, means the model is
  working in that chat. A dot means it stopped while you were in another chat:
  it finished, or it's waiting for you to allow something. The dot goes once
  you open the chat.
- Point at a tab to see the chat's profile.

## Profiles

A profile sets how a chat starts: its system prompt, its author's note, its
tools, and its [model](#the-chats-model). The first of the chips under the
message box names the chat's profile. Click it and choose another, and the chat
takes that profile's settings in place of its own. The same menu
[makes, renames and deletes](#manage-profiles) profiles.

New chats start on the project's **Default chat profile**, set in
[Project settings](/projects/#project-settings). A project that hasn't chosen
one uses the profile with the star in the profile menu.
Chats on a character card start on **Profile for character cards** in
**Settings** › **Extensions** › **Roleplay**.

- **Default**: brainstorm and revise, with the run of the project.
- **Roleplay**: a scene with one character, in their voice. The model can use
  the project's documents and nothing else. Offered while the Roleplay
  extension is on; see [Roleplay](/roleplay/#turn-on-roleplay).
- **Roleplay (NSFW)**: Roleplay, with the explicit themes you opt into in its
  author's note. Turn on **Enable NSFW chat profiles** in **Settings** ›
  **Extensions** › **Roleplay** to use it. It then takes Roleplay's place, and
  chats on Roleplay run on it until you turn the switch off.
- **Blank**: no system prompt, no tools, no project. The model as it comes.

### Save changes to a profile

A chat's system prompt and author's note are that chat's only. Its
[model](#the-chats-model) and [tools](#tools) aren't: changing them in a chat
changes them for its profile's next chats too. A built-in skill's wording,
under **Skills** in [Settings](#chat-settings), is its profile's: changing it
in any chat changes it for every chat on the profile.

To start new chats with the chat's system prompt or author's note, click the
disk beside its name, under **Prompts** in [Settings](#chat-settings). It shows
only while the text differs from its profile's, beside the arrow that takes the
profile's back.
Chats already on the profile keep their own settings. Saving to a built-in
profile changes it in place. Choosing the chat's own profile again in the
profile menu puts the whole chat back as its profile has it.

### Manage profiles

The profile menu, from the profile chip under the message box, lists every
profile. The chat's own is tinted.

- The star marks the profile new chats start on where a project hasn't chosen
  its own. Right-click another and choose **Set as default** to start them
  there.
- **New profile…** asks for a name, makes a profile from this chat's settings,
  and moves the chat onto it.
- Right-click a profile of your own to **Rename…** or **Delete** it. Deleting
  one sends its chats back to the project's default.
- **Prompts…** opens the chat's prompts in [Settings](#chat-settings).

## The chat's model

The second chip under the message box names the chat's model, with its
connection and how hard it thinks. Click it for the chat's **Model** and its
**Reasoning effort**. Each is this chat's, and the next
new chat on its profile starts with it too; chats already going keep their own.
A chat moved to a stronger model for a hard question leaves your other chats
where they were.

**Model** lists the chat's model and the ones you chose lately. **Other
models…** opens **Settings** › **Models**, the
[list of models](/providers/#models), where a click on any model of your
connections moves the chat onto it. **Reasoning effort** lists only the levels
the model takes, and says _None_ for a model with nothing to choose.

A chat keeps the model it first answered on.

The last chip says **Suggesting** or **Editing**: whether the model's changes
to documents wait for you. See [Document edits](#document-edits).

## Chat settings

The rest of a chat's settings are in **Settings**, the gear at the bottom left.
While a chat is showing, its profile is at the top of the list of sections,
with the same [menu](#manage-profiles) as the chip under the message box, and
**Prompts**, **Models**, **Tools** and **Skills** are that chat's.

<Screenshot name="chat-settings" alt="Settings, open at a chat's tools" />

- **Prompts**:
  - **System prompt**, which tells the model what it's there to do. It starts
    as the profile's; the reset button beside it goes back to the profile's.
  - **Author's note**, this chat's own, which goes with each message you send.
    Put standing instructions here: a tense, a point of view, things to avoid.

  **Open in editor**, beside either one, opens it as a tab in the editor.
- **Models** is the [list of models](/providers/#models): click one to move
  the chat onto it. The button at the end of a model's card opens its
  settings, which reach every chat on that model.
- **Tools**, **Skills** and **Roleplay** are below.

### Roleplay

**Roleplay**, under **Prompts**, holds what a chat on a
[character card](/roleplay/) brings with it. It shows in roleplay chats: chats
on a card, and chats on Roleplay or on the **Profile for character cards**. It
belongs to the chat: saving a prompt to the profile leaves it out, and
switching profiles keeps it.

- **User** and **Character** are the names `{{user}}` and `{{char}}` become in
  what the model reads. Left empty, the model reads the macro as written. A new
  name counts from your next message; what's been said keeps the old one.
- **System prompt** goes after the chat's system prompt, and **Post-history
  instructions** after its author's note. A chat on a card starts with the
  card's. Where either says `{{original}}`, the chat's own goes there instead.

### Tools

**Tools** lists what the model can use, in groups by where each comes from.
Switch off a group, or one tool in it, to keep it from the model; hover a tool
to see what it does. A switch here is this chat's, and the next new chat on
its profile starts with it too; chats already going keep their own.
**Enable tools** switches them all off, and back on as each was switched.

Something you add, a search service, an [MCP server](/mcp/) or
[Python](/python/), is on in every chat, except on the Roleplay and Blank
profiles, which offer only what they start with. A tool that asks before each
call says **Asks first**, and one you've let run without asking says **Always
allowed**.

- **Documents**: listing, searching, reading, creating, editing, renaming,
  moving and deleting the project's documents,
  [exploring](#explore-the-project) them, and reading and resolving your
  [comments](/comments/). What the model deletes goes to the
  [trash](/projects/#delete-and-the-trash). To let the model read but not
  write, leave **Read document**, **Describe document**, **List documents**,
  **Search documents** and **Explore documents** on, and switch off the rest.
- **Web**: searching the web and reading pages, once you've connected a search
  service. See [Web Search](/web-search/).
- **Python**: running code, in the desktop app, once you've turned it on. See
  [Python](/python/).
- Each [MCP server](/mcp/) you've connected has a group of its own.
- **Skills**: each of your [skills](/skills/) the model can call.
- **RPG Tools**: dice, the oracle, tables, tarot and names, for games.

If the switches are greyed out, the chat's model can't call tools, or **Allow
tool use** is off under **Generation** in its
[settings](/providers/#models).

### Document edits

The last chip under the message box says whether the model's changes to
documents wait for you. It's this chat's, and new chats on its profile start
with it. The chip is there while the chat has its **Documents** tools.
**Editing** makes the model's changes at once; **Suggesting** has it ask
first:

- Its changes to a document's text are [suggestions](/comments/#suggestions)
  in the text, each change on its own, for you to accept or reject there or in
  the **Comments** tab.
- A new document, a delete, a comment it leaves or resolves, and a change to a
  document in the [plain text editor](/editor/#the-plain-text-editor) wait in
  the chat, showing what goes and what comes in, until you click **Accept** or
  **Reject**.

A move, a rename or a new folder is made at once.

When the model adds to the end of a document you have open, its words appear
there as it writes them, as a suggestion if it's suggesting. They go into the
document once it has finished writing them. If you stop the reply before
then, nothing goes in.

### Skills

**Skills** lists every [skill](/skills/): the built-in ones, then your own.
Whether the model can call one is under **Tools**. Open a built-in skill to
change its **Instructions** for the chats on this chat's profile
([more](/skills/#reword-a-built-in-skill)).

### Voices

Once a speech service is set up for [narration](/narration/), **Read aloud**
on a turn reads it. Right-click **Read aloud** and choose a voice under **Set
voice**: on a reply, for the model's turns in this chat; on a turn of yours,
for your own, which are read in the model's voice until you choose another.
**Narration settings…**, under the voices, opens the narration panel, where
voices are made.

## What the model sees of your project

The model doesn't get the whole project with every message. It gets the
project's name and [summary](/projects/#project-settings), and finds the rest
itself: it lists, searches and reads documents as it needs them. Click the **tool calls** box above a reply to
see what it looked at; a path there opens that document, and so does a link to
a document in a reply. To point the model at
a document, type `[[` and the start of its name in your message and pick it
from the list, or drag it from the outline into your message, or right-click
it, choose **Copy link**, and paste it there: it shows as a link to the
document, and the model reads the document's path. A `#` after the name lists
its headings, as in a document. The list has folders too, for the model to
list.

### Attach documents

To put a document in front of the model without waiting for it to read it,
attach it: type `@` and the start of its name in your message, and pick it
from the list. Its link goes into your message with the `@` before it, and the
whole document goes to the model with the message, as it stands when you send
it. The list says how many words each document has, since all of them are
sent.

An attached document stays in the conversation as it was sent. When it changes
later, the model is told so, and reads it again when it needs to.

### Hide documents

Hover a document or a folder in the outline and click the eye to hide it from
the open chat: the model can't list, search or read it. On a phone, long-press
it and choose **Hide from this chat**. To hide one from every chat,
right-click it and choose **Hide from all chats**.

### Explore the project

In a large project, the model can't read everything to find what it needs.
Exploring has it read a folder, or the whole project, and write a line on what
each document and folder is about. In every chat, the model then sees those
lines when it lists the project, and knows where to look. A line counts until
its document changes, and exploring again reads only what has changed.

- Type `/explore` for the whole project, or `/explore Notes` for a folder or a
  document. Your turn says how far it has got, then what the folder holds,
  with a line on each document in it, which the model reads too.
- The model can explore too, when it needs to find its way around. What it
  found shows in its reply.
  It runs on the chat's model. Each request is paid for like a chat message, so it
  asks first when it will read more than about 50,000 tokens, saying about how
  many. **Always allow**, beside the model's call, stops it asking, for good.
  What's hidden from all chats, [repositories](/repositories/), and files with
  no text are left out.

## A turn's buttons

- **Copy**, and **Read aloud** once a speech service is set up for
  [narration](/narration/). Right-click **Read aloud** to choose its
  [voice](#voices).
- **Edit turn** or **Edit message**, the pencil, changes what was said, without
  asking for a new reply. <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>
  <kbd>Enter</kbd> on Windows and Linux) saves, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> cancels.
- **Regenerate reply**, the circular arrow on a reply, asks the model again.
  **Send again**, the arrow on your turn, asks again for its reply, or for one
  where there is none.
- **Previous answer** and **Next answer** step through the last reply's
  answers, as _2 / 3_. Each answer's changes to documents come and go with it.
- **⋯** holds **Show raw** (the turn as the model is sent it), **Summarize up
  to here** ([below](#compact-a-long-chat)), **Delete turn** or **Delete
  message**, and:
  - **Fork from here**: copies the chat up to that turn into a new chat and
    opens it in a tab beside this one. It's named with _- forked_ on the end
    until the model names it from the first thing you say in it. To fork the
    whole chat and say something there at once, use
    [`/fork`](#commands).
  - **Rewind to here**: after asking, removes the turns after it and undoes
    their changes to documents, except passages you've edited since.

<Screenshot
  name="chat-turn-menu"
  alt="A reply's buttons, its other answer, and its menu"
/>

Regenerating an earlier reply removes the turns after it and undoes their
changes to documents, except passages you've edited since. To keep them,
**Fork from here** and regenerate in the fork.

A reply's header names its model and, as **ctx**, the size of the conversation
it was sent; hover it for the tokens. **Thought for**, above a reply, opens the
model's thinking. A reply that fails says why, with **Retry**.

## Commands

Type `/` at the start of a line for the commands. Keep typing to narrow the
list; <kbd>Tab</kbd> or <kbd>Enter</kbd> finishes the name, and <kbd>Esc</kbd>
closes the list. The sparkles mark the ones that call the model.

| Type                                         | And                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/roll 3d6+2`                                | It rolls the dice. An `a` adds an advantage die and drops the lowest, so `3d6a` rolls four d6s and keeps the best three, and `d20a+1` keeps the higher of two d20s. A `b` adds a burden die, or disadvantage, and drops the highest. `3d6a2` adds two dice and drops two. Dice can be added to, taken from, multiplied and divided, as in `2d6-1d6`, `5d4*10` or `8d6+4 / 2`, which rounds down. What's written without spaces counts as one roll, so `3d6+1 * 2` doubles all of it and `3d6 + 1 * 2` only the 1. Add a question after them, as in `/roll 2d6 How does the monster react?`, and the model reads the roll for it. |
| `/oracle(likely) Is the door locked?`        | It answers yes or no at the odds you give: nearly impossible, very unlikely, unlikely, 50/50, likely, very likely or nearly certain. Leave them off for 50/50.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `/roll-table "a knife" "a letter" "nothing"` | It picks one of the options.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `/tarot(1, full) Will she forgive him?`      | It draws cards: 3 from the major arcana unless you say how many (up to 10) or `full` for the whole deck. The question is optional.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `/name(female, 3)`                           | It makes up names, `female` or `male`, up to 25.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `/director Wrap this scene up.`              | It tells the model what the next reply needs.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `/fork What should happen next?`             | It copies the chat into a new tab and sends the message there, so the chat you were in goes on as if it was never asked. `/fork` alone just makes the copy.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `/explore Notes`                             | The model writes a line on what each document in the folder is about, to find things by. Leave the folder off for the whole project; [see above](#explore-the-project).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `/compact`                                   | The model summarizes the chat; [see below](#compact-a-long-chat).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

A command's answer goes into your turn, and the model takes it as settled;
`/compact` answers in a turn of its own. A message of nothing but
commands gets no reply, so you can settle things before you say what you do;
your own [skills](/skills/#make-your-own) do ask for one.
`/fork` is a message of its own, and everything after it, lines and all, goes
to the fork.
**Ask again** on an answer asks again, and **Edit command** changes the
question but keeps the answer. A roll shows its dice above its total, a line
for each set, with the ones it dropped first and struck through. **Edit turn**
has them as text, the dropped dice between `~~`: change a die and the total is
worked out again, or change only the total to keep your own number. Your own [skills](/skills/), and the prompts of
[MCP servers](/mcp/) you've connected, are in the list too.

## Compact a long chat

Every turn sends the whole chat so far, so a long chat gets slower and costs
more, and can outgrow what the model can read at once. Type `/compact` and the
model writes a summary to stand in for the turns before it. The turns stay for
you to read, dimmed under a line that says how many were compacted. The chat's
first message always stays as it is.

- `/compact(4)` keeps the last four turns as they are, under the summary.
- Words after it say what to keep: `/compact Keep the heist; the tavern can go.`
- **Summarize up to here**, in a turn's **⋯** menu, summarizes everything above
  that turn.

Each summary folds in the one before, so the newest tells the whole story.
Edit it like any turn. Delete it, and the model goes back to the summary
before, or to the whole chat. To change how a profile summarizes, change
**Compact**'s **Instructions** under [Skills](#skills).

## Find in a chat

Press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd> on Windows and
Linux) in the chat, or click **Find in chat**, the magnifier at the top. It
finds in the whole chat, and works as [find](/editor/#find-and-replace) does in
the editor.
