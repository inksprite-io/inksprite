inksprite can read your documents aloud, in voices you choose. This guide reads
the first letter of Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein*, which is in the public
domain, with [Kokoro](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M), a free speech
model small enough to run on an ordinary computer.

## Run a speech server

[Kokoro-FastAPI](https://github.com/remsky/Kokoro-FastAPI) serves Kokoro on
your computer. With [Docker](https://www.docker.com/products/docker-desktop/)
installed, start it with:

```sh
docker run -p 8880:8880 ghcr.io/remsky/kokoro-fastapi-cpu:latest
```

The first start downloads it, which takes a while. With an NVIDIA graphics
card, this one is faster:

```sh
docker run --gpus all -p 8880:8880 ghcr.io/remsky/kokoro-fastapi-gpu:latest
```

Its [README](https://github.com/remsky/Kokoro-FastAPI#readme) has other ways to
run it.

## Import the book

Download the **EPUB3** from
[*Frankenstein*'s page at Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/84).
In inksprite, make a project for it, right-click the project, choose
**Import** › **Files…**, and pick the file. You can drag the file onto the
project instead.

<Clip name="import-book" label="Importing Frankenstein" />

The book comes in as a folder named after the file, with a document for each
letter and chapter, and the EPUB itself at the end.

## Open the narration panel

Open **Letter 1** and click the speaker icon at the far left. The panel reads
the document in the editor a **segment** at a time: each paragraph is a
segment, and so is each heading.

<Screenshot name="narration-panel" alt="The narration panel beside Letter 1" />

## Choose a voice

The voice called **Narrator** reads everything. Open **Voices** to change it.

<Screenshot name="narration-voices" alt="The voice settings" />

At the top, **Provider** is already **Kokoro**, and with the server running,
**Model** shows `kokoro`. If your server isn't at `http://localhost:8880/v1`,
click **⋮** beside **Provider** and change the address. Each project keeps its
own.

:::note[Other speech services]
Narration can also speak through **OpenRouter** or **OpenAI**, with the key you
connected for the chat. Choose one under **Provider**, then a **Model**. Each
model has its own voices, listed under **Add a voice to the mix…**; OpenRouter
has Kokoro too, with most of its voices. Both charge for what they read.
:::

Below them is the voice itself:

- **Voice** is what Kokoro is asked for: one of its voices, such as
  `bm_george`, or several mixed with `+`. A number in brackets gives a voice
  more weight: the Narrator's to start with,
  `af_heart+af_nicole(2)+af_jadzia`, is half `af_nicole`. **Add a voice to the
  mix…** lists Kokoro's voices.
- **Speed** is how fast it reads, from 0.5 to 2.
- **Preview** reads a line in the voice.

To hear the voice on the letter itself, click the headphones (**Generate a
sample**). inksprite reads the first few segments, here as far as the second
paragraph, and you play them in the player above **Voices**.

## Fix what it reads wrong

The sample starts with the letter's dateline, *St. Petersburgh, Dec. 11th,
17—.* Kokoro reads *Dec.* as it's spelled, like *deck*, and the year, Shelley's
way of saying some time in the 1700s, doesn't read aloud well. **Pronunciation**
and **Filters** fix both. They change what Kokoro is sent; the document stays
as it is.

<Screenshot name="narration-fixes" alt="A pronunciation hint and a filter, and the filtered words greyed in the letter" />

### Pronunciation

Open **Pronunciation** and type a word, a colon, and what to say instead, one
to a line:

```
Dec.:December
```

A hint matches whole words, in capitals or not. Spell a name the way it
sounds (`Aelinor:AY-lin-or`), and click **Preview** to hear every hinted word
as Kokoro will say it.

### Filters

Open **Filters** and type what to leave out, one to a line:

```
, 17—.
```

Filtered text is greyed in the editor and in the list of segments, and isn't
read. With both fixes, the dateline reads *St. Petersburgh, December 11th*.

Each line is a [regular expression](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Guide/Regular_expressions),
so it can match a pattern: `^\d+$` leaves out page numbers on lines of their
own, and `\[\d+\]` footnote marks like *[12]*. A full stop matches any
character, so write `\.` for a full stop alone. A segment filtered out whole is
skipped.

## Generate the letter

Click **Generate** (the sparkles). inksprite sends the segments to Kokoro one
at a time and shows how far it has got. **Stop** takes its place while it
runs; what it made before you stopped stays.

<Clip name="narration-generate" label="Generating the whole letter" />

When it's done, play the letter in the player, or from any segment with the ▷
at the start of its row.

Generate only reads segments that have no audio yet, so after you change a
paragraph, a voice, a hint or a filter, it reads just the segments that
changed. To read segments again:

- **One**: click ↻ at the end of its row.
- **Some**: click them to select them (<kbd>Shift</kbd>-click for a run,
  <kbd>⌘</kbd>-click or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>-click to add one), then **Generate**.
- **All**: click **Regenerate all**, the ↻ beside the headphones.

## Download it

Click the download button, last in the row, for a WAV file of the whole
document. WAV files are large, about 3 MB a minute: this letter is 23 MB. Any
audio converter makes an MP3 of it a sixth of the size.

:::caution[Download what you want to keep]
In a browser, the audio stays only while inksprite is open. Close the tab or
reload the page and it's gone; Generate makes it again, and on OpenRouter or
OpenAI is paid for again. The desktop app keeps it with the project when
**Settings** › **System** › **Keep projects in a folder** is on.
:::

## More than one voice

Each segment can have a voice of its own. Add a voice with **+** under
**Voices**, then click the person icon on a segment's row and choose it; with
segments selected, the choice goes to them all. Give a voice a **Colour** to
show its segments in that colour in the editor. The palette button at the top
of the panel turns the colours off.

A segment is a whole paragraph, so this suits writing where each paragraph has
one speaker, like a script or an interview, but not a novel's dialogue, where
what's said and *she said* share a paragraph.
